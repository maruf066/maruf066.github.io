Boeing 737 Nose Landing Gear Light Bracket: Static, Modal, and Harmonic Response Analysis

This project presents a comprehensive finite element analysis of a Boeing 737 nose landing gear light bracket using COMSOL Multiphysics. The geometry was based on dimensional measurements taken from the actual aircraft and modeled in Aluminum 7075-T6.

Three analyses were performed:

  • Static: Under a 500 N load, the maximum von Mises stress was 120.3 MPa with a safety factor of 4.2, and the maximum displacement was 0.2 mm. The equilibrium error was 0%.
  • Modal: The first six natural frequencies ranged from 353.13 Hz to 985.35 Hz, all safely above typical aircraft vibration levels.
  • Harmonic: With 2% damping, a 1–300 Hz sweep showed no resonance peaks, confirming dynamic stability.

Subsonic Flow Field Analysis of a NACA 0012 Swept Wing

Swept wings are widely used in high-speed aircraft to increase the critical Mach number and reduce drag. This project performs a subsonic flow field analysis of a 30° swept NACA 0012 semi-span wing using ANSYS Fluent.

A density-based solver and the Spalart-Allmaras turbulence model are employed to simulate the external aerodynamic flow.

Objectives & Validation:

  • Visualize the pressure and velocity distributions over the wing.
  • Evaluate the aerodynamic coefficients (lift and drag).
  • Numerical results are validated against published experimental data for the NACA 0012 airfoil.

This project focuses on developing an automated safety system to prevent accidents caused by fatigue among security personnel. Using computer vision and machine learning algorithms, the system monitors eye movement and facial landmarks in real-time to detect signs of drowsiness.

Upon detecting drowsiness, the system triggers an audible alarm to alert the guard, ensuring continuous vigilance and safety.

Key Contributions:

  • Implemented facial landmark detection using Python and OpenCV.
  • Developed the drowsiness detection algorithm based on Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR).

NACA 4421 Airfoil — Slot and Groove Study

This independent project investigates the aerodynamic impact of slot and groove modifications on a NACA 4421 airfoil. Using ANSYS Fluent, computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations are performed to analyze variations in lift, drag, and overall flow behavior over the modified airfoil surfaces.

The study aims to determine whether these passive flow control mechanisms can effectively delay boundary layer separation and improve aerodynamic efficiency.
